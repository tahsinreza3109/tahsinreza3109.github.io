About

Most of the interesting problems in Dhaka's traffic are behavioral, not geometric.

I graduated in Urban and Regional Planning from BUET in June 2026 with a CGPA of 3.70 out of 4.00. My work sits at the junction of travel behavior and road safety in mixed, heterogeneous traffic, and I approach it with explainable machine learning.

Two strands run through it. The first is travel behavior: how a trip gets assembled, and what decides whether it stays on one mode or chains several. The second is what happens once those vehicles reach the road, which I have now approached from different angles: my undergraduate thesis, a TRB conference manuscript under review, and a third article in preparation, each pushing how much the same dataset can be made to explain. That work also involved building an AI-assisted traffic data extraction pipeline with YOLO26 detection and tracking for the TRB manuscript.

Alongside this sit studio projects across rural, participatory, regional and landscape planning, and professional practice at the Dhaka Transport Coordination Authority and Sheltech Consultants.

I am looking for opportunities to continue my studies: a graduate programme in transportation planning and data science, where I can keep working where behavior, safety and policy meet.

Research Experience

2025 – 2026 Dhaka, Bangladesh

Time Headway Behavior of Pedal Rickshaws & Battery-Operated Three-Wheelers

Department of Urban and Regional Planning, BUET · Supervisor: Dr. Md. Musleh Uddin Hasan · with Dr. Md Asif Raihan (Accident Research Institute, BUET)

Battery-operated three-wheelers now share Dhaka's roads with pedal rickshaws in large numbers, but traffic models, and the policy horizon built on them, still treat the two as a single class. The work asks whether that assumption holds, and what follows for how the modes should be regulated.

  • Undergraduate thesis: videographic surveys manually extracted into vehicle-pair observations, then modeled with tree-based explainable machine learning, supported by extensive descriptive and inferential analysis.
  • TRB manuscript: built an automated extraction pipeline: a YOLO26 detector trained on roughly 207,000 self-labeled vehicle instances, paired with BoT-SORT tracking, producing about 900 follower–leader observations across two corridors.
  • Fitted pair-specific headway distributions and accelerated failure time models to explain the distributional characteristics and shifts, accounting for covariate effects that feed both simulation modeling and the policy horizon.
  • Manuscript in preparation, One Label, Two Vehicles: econometric and machine learning modeling to untangle flow characteristics from policy intervention.

Personal Research

Monomodal versus Intermodal Trip Choice in Dhaka: Bayesian Multilevel Models and Explainable Machine Learning

Manuscript in Preparation

Built a five-level (M0–M4) Bayesian multilevel logistic regression alongside an explainable Random Forest and SHAP pipeline to model monomodal versus intermodal trip choice across 25,242 trips nested within 3,729 Dhaka households. Household vehicle ownership emerged as the strongest driver of trip modality, and rickshaw-plus-walking as the dominant intermodal chain, 55 percent of the 6,838 intermodal trips, pointing to clear implications for paratransit and walking infrastructure policy.

Bayesian Multilevel Models Discrete Choice Random Forest SHAP

One Label, Two Vehicles: Time-Headway Evidence Against Treating Pedal and Battery Rickshaws Alike in Mixed Traffic

Manuscript in Preparation

A location-scale econometric decomposition, with machine learning support, separating why battery three-wheelers follow more closely than pedal rickshaws. Across two Dhaka corridors their time headway runs 30 to 39 percent shorter, but only about a tenth of that comes from taking less space: roughly two-thirds to four-fifths of the gap is carried by speed. Headways below two seconds occur in 22 to 29 percent of pedal rickshaw following events against 54 to 71 percent for battery three-wheelers, which separates a genuine capacity gain from the safety exposure that comes with it.
