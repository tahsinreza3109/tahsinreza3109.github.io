About

My work explores how people make travel decisions and how those decisions interact with safety in mixed-traffic streets. I bring together planning, spatial analysis, transportation modeling, data science, and intelligent transportation systems (ITS) perspectives to study mobility challenges through both human and technical lenses.

Through research, studio projects, and professional experience with public- and private-sector organizations, I have developed an interest in transportation planning that is analytical, practical, and people-centered. I am now seeking graduate opportunities in transportation planning and data science to continue working at the intersection of mobility, safety, behavior, technology, and policy.

Research Experience

Bachelor's thesis · 2025 – 2026

Flow Characteristics of Traditional Rickshaws and Battery-Operated Three-Wheelers in Non-Lane-Based Heterogeneous Traffic of Dhaka

Department of Urban and Regional Planning, BUET · Supervisor: Dr. Md. Musleh Uddin Hasan · with Dr. Md Asif Raihan (Accident Research Institute, BUET)

Battery-operated three-wheelers now share Dhaka's roads with pedal rickshaws in large numbers, but traffic models, and the policy horizon built on them, still treat the two as a single class. The thesis asks whether that assumption holds, and what follows for how the modes should be regulated.

Videographic surveys were manually extracted into vehicle-pair observations and modeled with tree-based explainable machine learning, supported by extensive descriptive and inferential analysis.

Intermodal versus Monomodal Trip-Making in Dhaka: Evidence from a Multilevel Choice Analysis

Built a three-level (M0–M3) Bayesian multilevel logistic regression alongside an explainable Random Forest and SHAP pipeline to model monomodal versus intermodal trip choice across 25,242 trips nested within 3,729 Dhaka households. Household vehicle ownership emerged as the strongest driver of trip modality, and rickshaw-plus-walking as the dominant intermodal chain, 55 percent of the 6,838 intermodal trips, pointing to clear implications for paratransit and walking infrastructure policy.

A participatory study of why a dense Dhaka ward stays vulnerable to earthquakes. Using vulnerability mapping, cause-effect diagrams and institutional Venn diagrams built with residents, the work traced how weak local governance and low public awareness reinforce each other.

Social and Physical Survey

Factors Influencing Access Mode Choice of Metro Rail Users in Dhaka

A survey-based study of how people reach the MRT-6 stations at Agargaon, Pallabi and Uttara. Rickshaw accounts for 41 percent of first-mile trips, and the mix shifts sharply by station and by surrounding land use. The analysis tests access mode against travel distance, waiting time, group size, income, gender, car ownership, road width and footpath coverage.

Read the report
Transport Planning Studio

Factors Influencing Pedestrian Catchment of MRT Stations in Dhaka

How far people actually walk to reach an MRT station, and what changes that distance. Mapped observed walking catchments around major stations in GIS, then used a predictive model to rank the land use and demographic factors that widen or shrink them.

Landscape Planning Studio

HydroSculpt: Alleviating Waterlogging through Landscape Planning

An idea book on treating waterlogging as a landscape problem rather than a drainage one. It maps where water collects and why, then proposes four connected strategies: buffer zones, a green network, permeable setbacks and pervious roads, with a planting palette to match.
